# What if, and why: tissue representations one can intervene on, interpret and test

<!-- v1 (2026-09-21). Draft for the Helmholtz Munich AIH PI call, track (i) AI in Biomedicine. Limit: 3 pages incl. figure. Citations use keys from research_plan.bib. Decisions locked in README.md. -->

Tissue foundation models now embed hundreds of millions of cells [@blampey2025novae; @tejadalapuerta2025nicheformer; @birk2026terra; @wenckstern2026virtues], yet none can be asked what a cell would do if its neighbourhood changed, and none can say what its features mean. Both limitations share an origin: these models are built and scored for representation quality, so an intervention on them is undefined and a feature in them is unnamed. Where *in silico* perturbation is offered, its validation is qualitative [@birk2026terra], and where features have been read with sparse dictionaries, they encode co-expression rather than regulation [@kendiukhov2026atlas], and no spatial model yet beats naive baselines on the local microenvironment [@handa2026saffron]. In our perspective on perturbation modelling [@dimitrov2026interpretation], we argued that this is why current models offer only partial views of causality: the microenvironmental cues that shape a cell's response are rarely measured and almost never modelled, and simple baselines consequently match foundation models on unseen conditions [@ahlmanneltze2025linear; @wu2024perturbench].

I will build tissue representations on which both questions are well-posed. A model whose counterfactuals hold in tissues it has never seen has captured something meaningful about how cells respond to their microenvironment; reading such a model, feature by feature, is how its predictions become targets one can test. I use "counterfactual" operationally: the predicted change in a cell's expression under a defined intervention on its context, tested by generalization to unseen contexts rather than by identification in the strict Pearlian sense.

**Where I start from.** My work on cell–cell communication provides the vocabulary and the evaluation culture for this programme. LIANA, which I created (sole first author, *Nat Commun* 2022; *Nat Cell Biol* 2024) [@dimitrov2022comparison; @dimitrov2024lianaplus], is the field's reference framework for inferring signalling between cells from single-cell, spatial and multi-omics data, downloaded more than 300,000 times, and rests on the prior-knowledge layer I helped build [@turei2021omnipath; @farr2024metalinksdb]. Its benchmark exposed how little communication methods agree, which led me to write the community's recommendations [@heumos2023bestpractices] and to lead the cell–cell communication task in Open Problems [@luecken2025openproblems]. Cellina (last and co-corresponding author) [@moeed2026cellina] then showed that tissue graph counterfactuals are tractable: with biological supervision as the inductive bias, a cell's intrinsic identity and its microenvironment separate, and the model is the only one to consistently outperform spatially-uninformed baselines on counterfactual prediction across 2.5 million cells. KIARA (first and corresponding; in preparation) approaches interpretability from the estimation side: it models what we know first, each cell's basal state and the pooled effect of each perturbation anchored in prior pathway knowledge, and only then the residual as interactions with cell type and niche, so that every recovered context effect is variation the additive stages could not explain.

## Build tissue representations that are intervenable and interpretable by design (Aim 1)

The central object of the programme is a generative model of tissue whose latent is structured rather than learned as an unstructured embedding. Following Cellina, each cell will carry an intrinsic representation anchored to its identity and an extrinsic representation of its microenvironment, learned from the tissue graph; following KIARA, perturbations and other contexts will enter as an explicit term expressed in a shared programme space, so that an intervention is an operation on the latent rather than a prompt to a black box. The ligand–receptor and pathway layer of LIANA and OmniPath will serve as the vocabulary in which interventions are specified and features are named. Prior knowledge remains an inductive bias, never a requirement: unsupervised disentanglement is ill-posed in general, but biology gives us the priors to make it well-posed in practice [@dong2025simvi; @piran2024biolord].

The model will be trained on observational spatial atlases together with the spatial perturbation screens that are now appearing [@dhainaut2022perturbmap; @gu2025crisprmap; @binan2025perturbfish; @shen2026spatialperturbseq], including Perturb-CAST, the in vivo combinatorial screen developed at DKFZ with my current colleagues [@breinig2026perturbcast]. Our perspective singled out these data as the next step for perturbation modelling: they measure the response of a perturbed cell and of its unperturbed neighbours in the same section, which dissociated screens cannot [@dimitrov2026interpretation]. I will start with transcriptomics and extend to spatial proteomics [@wenckstern2026virtues] once the architecture is fixed. I do not intend to compete on the number of cells; I intend to compete on what the representation can be asked.

## Query the representation counterfactually (Aim 2)

Two families of question will be supported, generalizing the tissue graph counterfactuals Cellina introduced. Node perturbations alter what a cell's neighbours express, as when a ligand is switched on or a pathway is treated in the cells around it. Cellina's validated node perturbations shift dense neighbour programmes; the extension will parameterize the intervention through the ligand–receptor layer, deleting a ligand in a given sender or dosing a cytokine, so that interventions compose and transfer across tissues through a shared vocabulary. TERRA and SpatialProp attempt such queries today [@birk2026terra; @sun2025spatialprop], but neither has been validated against measured responses in held-out tissue. Edge perturbations rewire the neighbourhood itself, as when a cell is placed into a different niche, the question MintFlow poses without priors [@akbarnejad2025mintflow]. Every query will be scored on held-out contexts and at gene level, against the linear and spatially-uninformed baselines the field has repeatedly failed to beat. Perturb-FISH has shown that neighbourhood density changes what a knockout does [@binan2025perturbfish]; whether a model predicts that change in a tissue it has never seen is the test for this aim.

## Interpret effects and features, and validate interpretations by intervention (Aim 3)

Interpretation will proceed at two levels. At the level of effects, KIARA will decompose an observed *in vivo* response to perturbation into a global, cell-type-specific term and a local niche term, with the additive stages fit first, so that the niche term is variance the global model could not explain. The output is a per-perturbation, per-programme answer to how much of an effect is the cell and how much its neighbourhood, expressed in the pathway vocabulary my prior-knowledge resources provide. At the level of features, I will learn sparse dictionaries over the Aim-1 latent and describe each feature with an autointerpretability pipeline: a blind language model proposes what a feature encodes, and an independent judge scores that description against prior knowledge, with no model ever seeing both the ground truth and the answer it grades [@bills2023neurons; @bricken2023monosemanticity; @templeton2024scaling; @paulo2025autointerp]. Sparse autoencoders have begun to expose features in single-cell and spatial foundation models [@pedrocchi2025saescfm; @kendiukhov2026celltype; @handa2026saffron]; I have built such an LLM-judged interpretability benchmark for clinical foundation models (ehrx; conceived and led by me, co-first and corresponding, in progress) and will port it to tissue. The two levels meet in one rule: an interpretation counts only if steering the feature in the Aim-1 model changes the counterfactual prediction. Features that are nameable and whose steering shifts the predicted response are the candidate targets this programme aims to deliver.

## Hold tissue representations to account (Aim 4)

Evaluation is where my previous work has had its widest effect, and it is where the group will start. I will build an Open Problems task series for tissue representations, evaluating Novae, Nicheformer, TERRA, VirTues and our own models on counterfactual prediction in held-out contexts, recovery of validated interactions, and interpretability. Ground truth will come from three sources. Interventional: spatial perturbation screens with held-out contexts [@breinig2026perturbcast; @gu2025crisprmap; @dhainaut2022perturbmap; @binan2025perturbfish]. Observational: datasets with experimentally validated interactions, found and curated by language-model agents across public repositories, extending the agentic curation of scBaseCount [@youngblut2026scbasecount] from expression matrices to validated communication events, with the extraction itself benchmarked so that its biases are measured rather than inherited. Mechanistic: the prior-knowledge layer, used for scoring interpretations, never as the sole ground truth for prediction. Every table will carry a spatially-uninformed baseline as a mandatory floor, and tasks will stay live rather than frozen. I would consider this aim successful if, within five years, external groups evaluate their tissue models on it, as they have used my previous tools.

## Fit and route to the clinic

The Computational Health Center is the natural home for this programme: Nicheformer and Open Problems were built here, and both are the collaborative surface for Aims 1 and 4. The institute's groups working from single cells and spatial data towards patients, on tumour neighbourhoods, and on pathology and microscopy images are the partners I envision for the second step of translation: because the representation is interpretable, patient sections can be summarized as niche-programme activities and related to outcome and treatment, and eventually read from routine pathology once spatial omics and H&E are aligned. The first disease setting will be colorectal cancer, where Cellina's 2.4-million-cell cohort and its TGFβ and NFκB/MAPK fibroblast programmes already exist. The output of Aims 2 and 3 is a ranked list of interventions whose predicted effect is nameable and holds in unseen contexts, a target-nomination engine for the institute's clinical partners, with the M1 Clinical AI Consultants as the route to prospective validation. I bring the DKFZ link to in vivo interventional data, the LIANA and Open Problems communities, and a funded CZI collaboration with the Saeys group (VIB–UGent), where I am a named key person.
