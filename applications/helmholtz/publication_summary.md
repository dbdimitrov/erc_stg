# Publication Summary — three most important papers

**1. Dimitrov D (sole first author), et al. Comparison of methods and resources for cell–cell communication inference from single-cell RNA-Seq data. *Nature Communications* 13, 3224 (2022). 700+ citations.**

I set up the LIANA framework and performed the comparisons and evaluations. This was the first work to systematically expose the limitations of cell–cell communication algorithms in single-cell transcriptomics: a comparison of 16 interaction resources and 7 inference methods showing that predictions depend strongly on both choices, that agreement between methods is low, and that no method is robustly supported by independent spatial or cytokine-signalling data. Building on it, I wrote the community's recommendations for cell–cell communication analysis in the Single-cell Best Practices book (Heumos et al., *Nat Rev Genet* 2023) and led the design of the cell–cell communication task in Open Problems in Single-Cell Analysis (Luecken et al., *Nat Biotechnol* 2025), co-authoring both. As such, this work seeded the LIANA software line and the commitment to evaluation that my proposed programme carries forward: from descriptive summaries of communication to learned representations of tissue that can be queried counterfactually.

**2. Dimitrov D (sole first author), et al. LIANA+ provides an all-in-one framework for cell–cell communication inference. *Nature Cell Biology* 26, 1613–1622 (2024). 300+ citations.**

I co-conceived the project, developed the software (liana-py), carried out the case studies, and drafted the manuscript. LIANA+ is the field's go-to Python framework for inferring inter- and intracellular signalling from single-cell, spatial and multi-omics data across conditions, a core scverse package, and downloaded more than 300,000 times. I built LIANA+ in steps, each addressing a limitation that the benchmark had exposed. Because no single method was reliable, LIANA+ re-implements the main ligand–receptor scoring methods behind one interface and combines them into a consensus. Because these methods only capture snapshots of single samples, I added strategies to represent communication across conditions (Baghdassarian*, Dimitrov*, Armingol* et al., *Cell Reports Methods* 2024; co-first author), which decomposes interactions across many samples into coordinated programmes that distinguish patients and disease states. Because communication is not only protein-mediated, I co-conceived and supervised MetalinksDB (Farr*, Dimitrov* et al., *Briefings in Bioinformatics* 2024; co-first author), a metabolite–protein knowledge graph an order of magnitude larger than existing resources, so that LIANA+ can infer metabolite-mediated signalling from spatial metabolomics and transcriptomics side by side. And because signalling does not stop at the receptor, LIANA+ links inferred interactions to downstream transcription factors and intracellular signalling networks. Together these make LIANA+ the only framework that represents communication consistently across dissociated, spatial and multi-omics data, and across conditions, within one prior-knowledge layer. The next step is LIANA++ (Alsayah et al., in preparation; conceived and supervised by me), which exploits the single-cell resolution of the newest spatial technologies to represent interactions at the level of individual cells, at each interaction's characteristic signalling range, and across patients.

**3. Moeed A, Schrod S, Rohbeck M, Bonder MJ, Lutsik P, Stegle O†, Dimitrov D†. Querying counterfactuals on tissue graphs with supervised disentanglement (Cellina). arXiv preprint (2026). *Submitted to ICLR 2027.* (†co-corresponding)**

Last and co-corresponding author (with O. Stegle); first-authored by a PhD student under my day-to-day supervision. In our recent perspective on perturbation modelling (Dimitrov†, Schrod† et al., *Nature Reviews Genetics* 2026), we argued that current models offer only partial views of causality, because the microenvironmental cues that shape a cell's response are rarely measured and almost never modelled, and that simple baselines consequently match specialist and foundation models on unseen conditions. Cellina targets exactly this gap. In this work, we formalized tissue graph counterfactuals: interventions on the two mutable components of a tissue graph. A node perturbation alters the expression of a cell's neighbours on a chosen set of genes, as when a ligand or pathway is switched on in the cells around it. An edge perturbation rewires the neighbourhood itself, as when a cell is transplanted into a new microenvironment, for example a healthy colon cell placed among tumour cells. Cellina makes both queries tractable by learning two representations for every cell, an intrinsic one anchored to cell identity and an extrinsic one capturing its microenvironment, with biological supervision as the inductive bias that separates the two. Benchmarked against state-of-the-art spatial perturbation and foundation methods across more than 2.5 million cells in observational and inteventional spatial omics data, Cellina is the only method that consistently outperforms spatially-uninformed baselines on counterfactual prediction, and it remains top-ranked on held-out spatial domains. Its disentangled spatial representation also reveals biologically distinct cancer subdomains without supervision, and pathway-targeted neighbour perturbations built from prior knowledge recover their responses. Beyond the method itself, Cellina shows that with the right inductive biases, spatial perturbations can be queried counterfactually. My proposed programme builds on exactly this: learning representations of tissue that can be intervened on, interpreted, and used to guide clinical targets and decisions.
