# <span id="span-9-3311815" class="ct-span">From Pixel to Patient: Making Medical AI Work in the Real World</span>

<span id="span-62-3311815" class="ct-span"><a href="https://dldnews.com/events/dld-health-2026/" rel="tag">DLD Health 2026</a></span>

<span id="span-217-3311815" class="ct-span">June 12, 2026</span>

<span id="span-68-3311815" class="ct-span"><a href="https://dldnews.com/topics/ai/" rel="tag">AI</a> \| <a href="https://dldnews.com/topics/innovation/" rel="tag">Innovation</a> \| <a href="https://dldnews.com/topics/medicine/" rel="tag">Medicine</a></span>

**Medical AI matters only when it moves from research into clinical use** through shared data, cross-institution work and tools that bring clear benefits to patients, this DLD Health expert panel, moderated by <a href="https://dldnews.com/speakers/michael-klimke/" data-type="speakers" data-id="3323479">Michael Klimke</a> (BAIOSPHERE), makes clear.

<a href="https://dldnews.com/speakers/julia-schnabel/" data-type="speakers" data-id="3325466">Julia Schnabel</a> (TUM/Helmholtz Munich) describes the field’s current transformation, in which **images are no longer treated as static, isolated data points** but as part of a patient’s individual health journey within a broader context. “The most exciting bit is that everything is starting to come together”, she says.

<a href="https://dldnews.com/speakers/carsten-marr/" data-type="speakers" data-id="3325450">Carsten Marr</a> (Helmholtz Munich/LMU) outlines **three opportunities for AI** in healthcare: mirroring what humans do but “quicker, more standardized and ideally cheaper.” Secondly, surpassing human capabilities in certain tasks. And thirdly, bringing expert knowledge and medical care to “low-resource settings”.

<a href="https://dldnews.com/speakers/jan-wopking/" data-type="speakers" data-id="3325595">Jan Wöpking</a> (M1) represents the Munich Medicine Alliance, a public foundation whose ambition is to turn Munich into “one of the **globally leading biomedical and biotech hubs** in the world”, he says. One of M1’s aims is to create a Munich Clinical Data Center and a Clinical Trial Center that make data and trials accessible to clinicians, researchers, and industry partners.
