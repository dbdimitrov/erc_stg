# Research Statement — Daniel Dimitrov

*Causal models of cell–cell communication: counterfactual inference on tissue graphs*

**Summary.** Perturbation-effect models today assume shared stimuli applied uniformly to i.i.d. cells — an assumption tissues violate by construction, because every cell's state is shaped by its neighbours, its niche, and the signals they exchange. My group will build the causal representation-learning stack for this setting: models that predict what a cell would do if its microenvironment were changed, with interventions parameterised mechanistically — which ligand, from which sender, through which receptor — mapping onto executable experiments. A model qualifies only if it predicts perturbation responses out-of-distribution (OOD), in tissue contexts it has never seen, measured against context-conditioned causal benchmarks the group builds. Two disciplines run through everything: rigorous evaluation, and infrastructure the community actually adopts — the two things AI-for-biology has too little of.

## Where I stand

I built and maintain infrastructure the cell–cell communication (CCC) field runs on. My systematic benchmark of CCC inference (sole first author, *Nature Communications* 2022; 706 citations [refresh]) showed that predictions across 16 interaction resources and 7 methods are strongly method- and resource-dependent, motivating the consensus approach the field has since adopted. LIANA+ (sole first author, *Nature Cell Biology* 2024) is the resulting framework for inter- and intracellular signalling across single-cell, spatial, and multi-omics data — downloaded more than 300,000 times [refresh] and independently adopted across the scverse ecosystem, alongside the wider OmniPath tool family. In total: 4,000+ citations, more than 1,000 of them to sole-first-author work [refresh].

I am equally frank about what this field has not delivered: descriptive CCC inference — my own tools included — has yet to produce a validated interventional result. Closing that gap is this programme's reason to exist. In a *Nature Reviews Genetics* Perspective (co-first, listed first, and co-corresponding author, 2025), I argued that single-cell analysis must move from atlasing to models that interpret, extrapolate, and predict perturbation effects — and that observational data yield only partial views of causality, making disentangled, invariant representations the route to transferable perturbation models.

My current work instantiates that agenda. **Cellina** (arXiv preprint, 2026 [refresh: arXiv ID]; senior and co-corresponding author with O. Stegle; first-authored by a PhD student I supervise day-to-day) disentangles a cell's intrinsic identity from its extrinsic microenvironment on tissue graphs — supervision as the inductive bias, chosen precisely because unsupervised factorisation is provably not identifiable — and formalises tissue-graph counterfactuals as edge perturbations (rewiring neighbourhoods) and node perturbations (altering neighbour programmes). Across benchmarks spanning over 2.5 million cells, including a 2.4-million-cell colorectal-cancer (CRC) cohort modelled per section, Cellina's best variant leads the strongest baseline by +0.17 on both Pearson and signed precision (0.85 vs 0.68; Pearson between predicted and observed perturbation-induced log-fold changes over top differentially expressed genes), far above the no-model mean-shift baseline (0.51) — margins consistent across patient slides, though within high cross-cell-type variance. **KIARA** (first and corresponding author) makes context effects themselves the deliverable through staged, null-anchored decomposition; the model and its six-dataset cross-validated benchmark suite are implemented, manuscript in preparation, preprint targeted early 2027 [refresh].

I use "counterfactual" operationally, not in the strict Pearlian sense: the estimand is the predicted change in a cell's expression under a defined intervention on its tissue context, evaluated by OOD transfer. And context effects in tissue are confounded — a healthy-to-tumour transfer partly scores tumour genetics, not microenvironment. Cellina's within-tumour control addresses exactly this: counterfactuals conditioned on the matched tumour subdomain outperform global-domain conditioning across all five cell types — same section, same genotype. Strand 1 makes such genotype-held-fixed arms standard.

## The programme: three strands

**Strand 1 — Context-conditioned causal benchmarks for CCC.** The field distrusts generalist perturbation models for good reason: simple baselines still match them, and no agreed ground truth exists for communication effects. This strand is deliberately first: it defines what "working" means before the models arrive. Genuine communication ground truth requires perturbation on the *sender* and readout in the *receiver* — scarcer than the Perturb-seq corpus. Day-one tasks build on existing data (cytokine and ligand dose panels, including the 20-cytokine donor screen already in KIARA's suite; co-culture screens with genotype-resolved partner readout); tasks needing richer sender-perturbed data are explicitly gated on Strand 3, which feeds the benchmark by design. Three structural safeguards: genotype- and clone-matched control arms (isogenic lines across co-culture compositions); a dedicated benchmark axis for *how much context effect is recoverable from domain identity alone* — in Cellina's mouse-brain cohort a non-spatial baseline nearly ties on correlation, and signed precision becomes the discriminating metric, a lesson the benchmark encodes; and gene-level, decision-grade metrics over distributional fit. LLM-mined ground truth is admitted only if the extractor is itself benchmarked — it echoes the same priors the models consume.

**Strand 2 — A generalist causal model of CCC via disentanglement and invariance.** Generalising Cellina from neighbourhood counterfactuals to communication counterfactuals: supervised disentanglement as the inductive bias, and the intervention variable parameterised through the ligand–receptor layer my infrastructure provides (LIANA+/OmniPath). This buys what generic perturbation embeddings cannot: interventions that *compose* (ligand sets, dose), *transport* across tissues through a shared ligand–receptor vocabulary, and carry sender→receiver structure — "delete ligand L in sender type S", not "resample the neighbour pool". The gap this creates is named, not hidden: Cellina's validated node perturbations are dense (100–200-gene) neighbour shifts, while a single-ligand intervention specified from prior knowledge alone is the sparse regime the wet lab lives in — measuring and closing that gap is a Strand-2 deliverable, not an assumed property. Invariance across environments is the identification lever, used knowing where it breaks — few, correlated environments, which tissue contexts are; the benchmarks are built to expose exactly that. A model qualifies only if it predicts perturbation responses in held-out contexts; a model that conflates intrinsic and extrinsic variation cannot pass.

**Strand 3 — Closed-loop validation in a context-dependent multicellular system.** Plate-based organoid/co-culture systems are the loop substrate — cycle times in weeks, hundreds of conditions per round automatable — with spatial profiling as periodic anchoring; I will pair with an experimental group from the start (I already hold a named key-person role in a funded CZI collaboration with the Saeys group, VIB–UGent). The primary handle is **gain-of-function**: recombinant ligand and cytokine dosing — no line construction, dose–response rather than binary outcomes, and the direction Cellina's pathway-vector perturbations tested in silico; ligand knockouts with add-back rescue are the confirmation step behind the gate, chosen with ligand redundancy in mind. The lead phenotype candidate: ligand-driven fibroblast programme activation — the TGFβ- and NFκB/MAPK axes Cellina flagged in CRC — read out by single-cell RNA-seq. The loop runs simulator-first: acquisition functions and query budgets stress-tested on existing perturbation corpora before a well is spent; wet-lab escalation is gated by a pre-registered threshold on decision-grade metrics (e.g., top-10 signed precision ≥ 0.5 on held-out benchmark contexts); any interaction clearing the gate is confirmed in an orthogonal modality (targeted smFISH or flow) so that segmentation error — which can dominate neighbour-influence analysis — never certifies a hit. The question the loop answers is the biological one: *which signal, delivered where, changes tissue behaviour?*

## (i) How my research interfaces with and benefits from AI

My research is method-building in machine learning. KIARA's core estimation idea: the control baseline is anchored to reproduce observed control cells; the pooled perturbation effect is fit and frozen before any interaction exists; every context term then starts at exactly zero effect and is shrunk back toward it — so any recovered context effect is signal the frozen stages demonstrably could not explain. Attribution is unambiguous by construction rather than left to the optimizer. Around it: graph-attention encoders whose attention readouts are validated against perturbation-based influence, and label-free contrastive objectives for niche representations. Supervised: Cellina, a deep generative model first-authored by a student under my direction. Applied: LIANA+ puts whatever the group builds in front of thousands of users.

My research also benefits from frontier AI. **ehrx** (conceived and led by me; co-first and corresponding; in progress [refresh]) asks what emerging clinical foundation models actually encode: a shared data substrate makes heterogeneous models comparable by construction, and an LLM-judged interpretability benchmark steers internal features, has a blind LLM interpreter describe them, and an independent judge score the descriptions against nulls, baselines, and an oracle ceiling — no LLM role ever sees both the ground truth and the thing it grades. Evidence that my agenda — interpretation over prediction — ports into clinical foundation models as a second domain.

Against AITHYRA's priority areas: depth today in **generative models** (Cellina; KIARA alongside) and **LLM-based evaluation** (ehrx; Strand 1's benchmarked extraction); **active learning and lab automation** are where the programme is heading, simulator-first, with acquisition under batch constraints. The sharpest methodological pieces go to ML venues as stand-alone contributions: the counterfactual-evaluation protocol as a datasets-and-benchmarks paper (NeurIPS D&B-track profile), the identifiability analysis of supervised disentanglement as a stand-alone ML paper.

## (ii) Scaling: what more data and compute buy

Next-generation spatial platforms (Xenium 5K, CosMx 18K-class panels) deliver transcriptome-wide measurement at single-cell resolution, and perturbation corpora keep compounding; my methods already operate per-section across millions of cells, and Strand 2's models are built to grow with each new tissue and perturbation context. The core scaling argument is about attribution: ambiguity about *what a model learned from where* compounds as models grow, so structured inductive biases — staged estimation, supervised disentanglement, invariance — become more valuable with scale, not less. And the evaluation scales in the same direction: as contexts multiply, held-out-context testing becomes stricter — the benchmark layer strengthens exactly when models are hardest to trust. What is achievable through scaling, on this programme's terms: a generalist model of multicellular systems whose interventional predictions are *earned* through benchmarked OOD accuracy rather than assumed from scale — and perturbation-guided therapeutic hypothesis generation with ligand/receptor-level handles in systems such as the tumour microenvironment.

## Why AITHYRA

What I add that no current group provides is evaluation and delivery infrastructure: an institute of model-builders needs one group whose discipline is defining what "working" means — and shipping the results in software the field already runs. I am direct about overlap: causal representation learning for cellular perturbations is shared ground with the institute's existing strength in machine learning for cell and tissue biology. The differentiation is technical, and it is a collaboration surface: communication as the modelled object, interventions that compose and transport through a shared ligand–receptor vocabulary, and the benchmark layer both groups' models should be measured on. With the institute's generative-dynamics expertise, the natural joint object is testable trajectories — generated dynamics under my counterfactual protocols. Tissue graphs will also need geometric structure beyond off-the-shelf message passing — boundary effects, local anisotropy, segmentation noise as a first-class error source — an open problem for the institute's geometric-deep-learning depth.

The campus supplies the Strand-3 systems (organoid, tumour-microenvironment, and immune models); AITHYRA's automation supplies the loop's other half. I bring an open-source track record — everything my group builds ships in tools people already use — supervision of five PhD and five MSc/BSc students, including Cellina's first author, and a funding plan whose centrepiece, an ERC Starting Grant submitted from AITHYRA in 2028, is built on this statement's strands.
