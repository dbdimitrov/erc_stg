Cell states in tissue are shaped by the signals cells exchange with their neighbours, and most perturbations act on whole tissues. Spatial omics now captures this context at single-cell resolution, and models increasingly aim to predict responses to perturbation.

Yet these models largely assume shared stimuli applied to independent cells, an assumption that does not hold in tissue. Modelling the microenvironment is challenging for three reasons: cell identity dominates transcriptomic variance while microenvironmental effects are a small fraction of it; those effects are entangled with cell type and perturbation; and, unlike either, they are largely unlabelled. Direct ground truth for communication (a perturbation applied to a sender with readout in a receiver) is even scarcer.

My group will instead build models that ask counterfactual questions of tissue: what would a cell do if its microenvironment were changed, which ligand from which sender would drive the change, and would the predicted response hold in unseen contexts? I use "counterfactual" operationally rather than in the strict Pearlian sense: the estimand is the predicted change in a cell's expression under a defined intervention on its context. The methodology is machine learning: generative models that disentangle a cell's identity from its context, estimation staged around prior knowledge, and benchmarks designed to test both. Communication cannot be measured in every context; generalization is therefore the key test, as a model that generalizes is a model that captures something meaningful about the underlying biology.

## Past and current research

**Benchmarks and infrastructure for cell–cell communication.** The growing interest in cell–cell communication (CCC) has produced more than 100 computational tools to infer it from single-cell data. To shed light on the impact of methodological choices, I systematically compared 16 interaction resources and 7 inference methods (sole first author, *Nature Communications* 2022). Top-ranked predictions showed relatively little overlap, yet most methods captured signals supported by orthogonal modalities. These results motivated a consensus approach and led to LIANA+ (sole first author, *Nature Cell Biology* 2024), an all-in-one framework extending these methods to spatial and multi-omics data. LIANA+ has been downloaded more than 300,000 times [refresh] and is part of the scverse ecosystem. I have also invested in community efforts: the CCC task in Open Problems (*Nature Biotechnology* 2025), the Single-cell Best Practices consortium, and the scverse hackathon I co-organized. My work has accumulated over 4,000 citations, more than 1,000 to sole-first-author papers [refresh].

**The limits of descriptive inference.** CCC inference from transcriptomics rests on co-expression, a proxy several steps removed from a communication event, and evaluations, including my own, rely on indirect ground truth such as co-localization. Descriptive CCC inference has yet to produce a validated interventional result; LIANA+, like other tools in the field, remains a tool for hypothesis generation. In a *Nature Reviews Genetics* Perspective (co-first, listed first, and co-corresponding author, 2026), I therefore argued that single-cell analysis must move beyond atlasing towards models that interpret, extrapolate and predict perturbation effects.

**Counterfactual queries on tissue graphs (Cellina).** Cellina (arXiv preprint, 2026 [refresh: arXiv ID]; senior and co-corresponding author with O. Stegle; first-authored by a PhD student I supervise) represents tissue as a graph and formalizes two families of *in silico* perturbations: edge perturbations, which ask what a cell would look like if its neighbours came from a different niche (a healthy fibroblast placed into a tumour microenvironment, say), and node perturbations, which ask how a cell would respond if its neighbours expressed a different programme. Supervised disentanglement separates each cell's intrinsic identity from its microenvironment, an inductive bias chosen because unsupervised disentanglement is ill-posed in general, but biology gives us the priors to make it well-posed in practice. Across benchmarks of more than 2.5 million cells, including a 2.4-million-cell colorectal cancer (CRC) cohort modelled per section, Cellina's best variant reaches a Pearson correlation of 0.85 between predicted and observed perturbation-induced expression changes, against 0.68 for the strongest baseline and 0.51 for a no-model mean shift. Context effects in tissue are confounded, as a healthy-to-tumour transfer partly measures tumour genetics. To account for this, we included a within-tumour control, in which counterfactuals conditioned on the matched tumour subdomain outperformed global conditioning for all five cell types tested, within the same section and genotype.

**Attribution by construction (KIARA).** KIARA (first and corresponding author; manuscript in preparation, preprint targeted early 2027 [refresh]) approaches the same problem from the estimation side: model what we know first, each cell's basal state and the average effect of each perturbation, anchored in prior pathway knowledge; only then model the residual as interactions between perturbation and microenvironment. Any recovered context effect is therefore variation the additive stages could not explain; attribution is built into the estimation itself. In preliminary analyses, KIARA's predictions are perturbation-specific (higher on-target than off-target accuracy in a large cytokine screen), and in a murine liver cancer model, accounting for the microenvironment improved prediction of held-out samples, with spatial effects that generalize to unseen tumours.

## Future research

The field is no longer limited by a lack of tools. Simple baselines still match perturbation-response models, and no agreed ground truth exists for communication effects, so predictions of communication cannot currently be falsified. To close this gap, the field needs a benchmark, models that can pass it, and experiments that confirm them; my group will pursue these as three aims. Aim 2 continues my current work; Aims 1 and 3 will begin with the group.

**Establish context-conditioned benchmarks for communication (Aim 1).** Initial tasks will build on existing cytokine and ligand dose panels and on co-culture screens with genotype-resolved readout; tasks requiring richer sender-perturbed data will follow from Aim 3. Every task will be scored on held-out contexts rather than on fit to a single dataset. The design will include three safeguards. First, genotype- and clone-matched control arms will ensure that context effects are not conflated with genetic differences. Second, a dedicated axis will quantify how much context effect is recoverable from domain identity alone: in Cellina's mouse-brain cohort, only signed precision separates spatial from non-spatial models. Finally, evaluation will rely on gene-level metrics relevant to experimental follow-up rather than to distributional fit. LLM-mined ground truth will be included only if the extraction itself is benchmarked, as it shares the biases of the models it evaluates.

**Generalize counterfactuals from neighbourhoods to communication (Aim 2).** Cellina's neighbourhood counterfactuals will be generalized to communication counterfactuals, with the intervention parameterized through the ligand–receptor layer my infrastructure provides (LIANA+, OmniPath): deleting a given ligand in a given sender cell type, instead of resampling a neighbour pool. This parameterization allows interventions to be combined and transferred across tissues through a shared ligand–receptor vocabulary.

One gap remains: Cellina's validated node perturbations shift dense, 100–200-gene neighbour programmes, whereas experiments typically target one or a few ligands specified from prior knowledge; whether accuracy transfers to that sparse regime is unknown, and measuring it will be among my group's first tasks. Invariance across environments will serve as the identification strategy, although tissue contexts are few and correlated, a failure mode the Aim-1 benchmarks will expose.

**Test predictions in a closed experimental loop (Aim 3).** Plate-based organoid and co-culture systems will form the experimental arm, offering cycle times of weeks, hundreds of automatable conditions per round, and periodic anchoring by spatial profiling. An experimental partner will be recruited on campus in the first year; the funded CZI collaboration with the Saeys group (VIB–UGent), where I am a named key person, provides a template for this loop. The primary intervention will be gain-of-function: recombinant ligand and cytokine dosing requires no line construction, yields dose–response rather than binary outcomes, and matches Cellina's *in silico* pathway-vector perturbations. A first candidate phenotype will be ligand-driven fibroblast programme activation along the TGFβ and NFκB/MAPK axes Cellina flagged in CRC.

The loop will first be run *in silico*: in each round the model will nominate the ligand, dose and context to test next, with acquisition functions and query budgets evaluated on the Aim-1 panels. Experiments will proceed only once a pre-registered threshold is met, for example a top-10 signed precision of at least 0.5 in held-out contexts. Interactions passing the threshold will then be confirmed in an orthogonal modality, targeted smFISH or flow cytometry.

## (i) How my research interfaces with and benefits from AI

Each aim rests on machine-learning method development: deep generative models with supervised disentanglement (Cellina); staged estimation anchored on additive null models, with attribution built in (KIARA); and, in both, graph-attention encoders with neighbour-influence readouts and contrastive niche objectives. These components raise two general machine-learning problems, counterfactual evaluation under distribution shift and identifiability of disentanglement on graphs; I will address both in dedicated ML papers, the evaluation protocol fitting a datasets-and-benchmarks track.

I also use large language models as instruments: in ehrx (conceived and led by me; co-first and corresponding; in progress [refresh]), a shared data substrate makes heterogeneous clinical foundation models comparable, and an LLM-judged interpretability benchmark evaluates what they learned; no LLM role sees both the ground truth and the answer it grades. Among AITHYRA's priority areas, my work aligns most closely with generative models and LLM-based evaluation; Aim 3 will additionally rely on active learning, in the form of sequential experimental design, and on the institute's laboratory automation.

## (ii) What is achievable through scaling

Foundation models have now reached spatial transcriptomics: Novae and Nicheformer (both *Nature Methods*, 2025) are trained on 30–110 million cells, and TERRA (2026, 112 million cells) extends this class of models towards *in silico* perturbation. Yet evaluations remain largely at the level of representation quality, and where perturbation is attempted, its validation is so far qualitative.

Counterfactual prediction in held-out contexts tests whether such representations capture transferable biology or dataset-specific structure. Aim 1 will supply the interventional evaluation layer these models currently lack; Aim 2's models can build on their representations where useful. As spatial panels reach transcriptome-wide coverage and the largest perturbation corpora, assembled for the virtual-cell efforts, exceed 100 million cells, the ambiguity about what a model has learned grows, and structured inductive biases become more important. However, those corpora profile dissociated cells under perturbations applied uniformly across the culture, not restricted to a sender with readout in an unperturbed receiver; scaling alone will not resolve the scarcity of sender-perturbed ground truth, which Aim 3 will address.

Progress will be measurable through methods and benchmark papers, and through adoption: I would consider Aim 1 successful if, within five years, external groups use it to evaluate their own models, as they have my previous tools. With that layer in place, I anticipate that scaling will bring a generalist model of multicellular systems within reach, its interventional predictions tested in held-out contexts, down to individual ligands and receptors in the tumour microenvironment.

## Fit with AITHYRA

I would bring to AITHYRA a complementary focus on evaluation and infrastructure the field already uses, and a tissue-scale perspective in a portfolio whose strengths lie at the molecular scale. My causal representation learning overlaps with the institute's group on machine learning for cell and tissue biology; I regard the overlap as an opportunity for collaboration, anchored by a benchmark layer both groups' models can be measured on; the same protocols would apply to the institute's generative models of cellular dynamics. Tissue graphs require geometric structure beyond off-the-shelf message passing (anisotropy, boundary effects, segmentation noise), where the institute's founding strength in geometric deep learning is directly relevant; causal learning, another institute priority, runs through Aims 1 and 2. The campus provides the multicellular systems required for Aim 3; closing the loop would be a collaboration with the institute's group on AI-driven autonomous scientific discovery and its lab-in-the-loop infrastructure. The centrepiece of my funding plan will be an ERC Starting Grant, to be submitted from AITHYRA in 2028 and built on Aims 2 and 3.
