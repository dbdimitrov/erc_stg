## How will artificial intelligence and bioinformatics change our understanding of IgA Nephropathy in the next decade?

*Bülow et al., Seminars in Immunopathology (2021) · **Author role: CO-FIRST author (with R.D. Bülow)***

**Problem:** IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis, yet its pathophysiology is only partially understood (the "4-hit" Gd-IgA1 hypothesis leaves open questions on B-cell origin, complement, microbiome, and progression heterogeneity). No specific treatments exist. Dissecting this systemic, multi-organ disease requires integrated analysis across molecular, cellular, and organ scales, generating "big data" that demand advanced computation.

**Approach:** A review organized in two halves. First, it surveys big-data experimental technologies for IgAN; second, it introduces AI/bioinformatics concepts and their applications, moving from general nephrology to nephropathology to the sparse IgAN-specific literature. IgAN is framed as a paradigmatic case for multidisciplinary integration of experimental, computational, and clinical expertise.

**Key contributions:** A structured map linking data-generation technologies to the computational methods needed to interpret them, and a candid catalogue of current limitations (small ethnically uniform cohorts, retrospective designs, missing external validation, absence of ground-truth consensus definitions). It advocates "augmented intelligence" (human + machine) and multi-organ, multi-omics, spatially-aware integration as the path to patient stratification and precision medicine.

**Data & tools:** Omics (GWAS, bulk/single-cell RNA-seq, proteomics, metabolomics, microbiome 16S/shotgun metagenomics); tissue imaging (MELC, Exchange-PAINT, CODEX, IMC, MALDI-MSI, 3D clearing/microCT, non-invasive radiology); ML/DL (CNNs for glomerular/multiclass segmentation on NEPTUNE, RNNs for AKI prediction, MMDx transplant classifier, KPMP consortium data). Discusses supervised/unsupervised/reinforcement learning, AUROC vs AUPRC, federated learning, and open challenges.

**Relevance to future work:** Establishes Daniel's early framing of disease biology as a computational integration problem spanning omics, spatial imaging, and clinical prediction — a through-line to his later single-cell and knowledge-integration work. Useful grounding for any kidney-disease or multi-modal-integration project, and a reference for the methodological caveats (bias, validation, causality vs correlation) that recur across his output.
