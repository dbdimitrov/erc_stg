# ehrx: Reading emerging EHR foundation models — one data substrate, one interpretability benchmark

*Planned submission: Nature Machine Intelligence · target: preprint concurrent with journal submission, mid-2027 · **Author role: co-first + corresponding — conceived, supervised, and led; the candidate's AI leadership-and-breadth pillar** · Status: work in progress; autointerpretability pillar mature, cross-model benchmark machinery complete, head-to-head numbers pending · Code: [github.com/PMBio/ehrx](https://github.com/PMBio/ehrx/tree/ehrdata) (branch `ehrdata`).*

**Problem:** Autoregressive EHR foundation models — Delphi-2M (Shmatko et al. 2025), MOTOR (Steinberg et al. 2023), SurvivEHR (Gadd et al. 2026) — now forecast disease trajectories from raw patient histories at scale. Two things stand between them and trustworthy use. First, they are **mutually incomparable**: each arrives welded to its own cohort, tokenization, binary format, and evaluation recipe, so any cross-model claim confounds the modeling objective with the pipeline it ships in. Second, **nobody knows what they encode**: clinical adoption hinges on whether internal features capture clinically meaningful structure (comorbidity patterns, disease mechanisms) or shortcuts (age, demographics) — and the interpretability tooling built for LLMs has no EHR-native equivalent, no ground truth, and no agreed way to score an explanation.

**The idea — two pillars:** ehrx makes emerging EHR foundation models *comparable* and *interpretable*. Pillar 1 is a **shared data substrate**: one canonical EHRData cohort object per dataset becomes the single source of truth from which every model's native training data, evaluation cohort, and label space are *derived* — so heterogeneous FMs can be trained and fairly evaluated cross-model and cross-dataset, with coherence enforced structurally rather than aspirationally. Pillar 2 is an **LLM-judged autointerpretability benchmark**: steer one internal feature at a time, let a blind LLM describe what it encodes, and let an independent judge verify that description against controls — nulls, no-explanation baselines, and a privileged-oracle ceiling — so "what does this feature encode" gets a measured answer, not an anecdote.

## Framework

**Pillar 1 — the EHRData substrate (cross-model, cross-data training and evaluation).** The canonical cohort is a **3-D EHRData tensor** — patients × ICD-10 codes × age-in-days, sparse COO, with recurrence living on the time axis — built once from raw MIMIC-IV 3.1 and carrying everything downstream stages must agree on: the vocabulary (`var` positions are *the* canonical risk axis every model's outputs map back onto), covariates with a frozen bucket spec defined in exactly one place, per-patient temporal cutoffs, and patient-level splits. Thin per-model exporters derive each FM's native format from it — Delphi's `.bin` plus a `layout.json` contract (vocabulary size and loss-mask derived from the data, missing file = hard error, no silent fallbacks), SurvivEHR's FastEHR parquet with the *same* covariate buckets as one-hots — so both models consume identical information by construction. A custom sparse codec makes the 3-D tensor round-trip through `.h5ed`, filling a real gap in the community stack (written to be upstreamed to ehrdata/anndata — this extends an emerging community standard in the AnnData/scverse lineage rather than inventing a private format). The head-to-head evaluation is **fairness-audited**: scored patients come only from a held-out split that contributed zero gradients to either model; the primary metric is windowed marginal AUROC over per-disease onset; an age/sex baseline is a mandatory floor in every table; and the audit's conditioning fixes (identical landmark histories for both models, a shared Monte-Carlo clock, best-validation checkpoints on both sides) are landed in code, not promised.

**Pillar 2 — autointerpretability with controls.** For each internal feature (of a probe or of the raw model), ehrx steers that feature and records how every disease's next-event log-odds shift. A **blind LLM interpreter** — the steered disease hidden, the prompt hardened to be sign-neutral so recovery cannot be a restatement of the trivial argmax rule — writes down what the feature encodes. A **matching judge** then scores every (explanation, code-set) pair into a matrix: the diagonal-vs-off-diagonal AUROC is the faithfulness headline, validity-gated by a no-explanation baseline and normalized between a shuffled-null floor and a privileged-oracle ceiling; recovery is separately graded against the no-LLM argmax baseline and a shuffled-Δ null. The design invariant that keeps the benchmark honest: **no LLM role ever sees both the ground truth and the thing it is grading.** Comorbidity-graph coherence (are the moved diseases graph-neighbours of the steered one?) is reported as a coordinate, never used as a grader. The whole pipeline is engineering-grade: pluggable LLM clients (a deterministic offline client for tests, live clients for results), bounded-parallel execution bit-identical to the sequential path, full per-call transcripts, and a self-contained interactive explorer dashboard rendered per run.

**The probes.** The feature spaces being graded span raw model embeddings (a near-null control), ICA, L1 and **Top-K sparse autoencoders** — the strongest probe so far — and a covariate-aware SAE variant; representation quality is additionally scored with DCI disentanglement metrics on Delphi and MOTOR features. The framework is probe-agnostic by design: the contribution is the substrate and the benchmark, not any single dictionary-learning method.

**Evidence to date (demonstrated in the repo):** the autointerpretability benchmark run end-to-end on Delphi — recovery and faithfulness metrics separate trained probes from nulls and baselines, Top-K SAE features are the strongest and raw-embedding steering sits near the null control; DCI disentanglement evaluations executed on both Delphi and MOTOR representations; steering and comorbidity analyses on Delphi; the full substrate (canonical MIMIC-IV cohort, both exporters, the fairness-audited evaluation harness) built and verified. **Planned before submission:** the retrained Delphi-vs-SurvivEHR head-to-head numbers on the shared MIMIC cohort; counterfactual evaluations; across-layer analyses; comorbidity analyses across additional cohorts; extending the substrate beyond first-occurrence diagnoses to the modalities the models natively consume (medications, valued measurements).

## Status & open items (firewall: do not let downstream narratives overclaim)

- **Demonstrated now:** the autointerpretability benchmark with results on Delphi (Top-K SAE strongest, raw embeddings ≈ null); DCI evaluations on Delphi and MOTOR; the canonical EHRData cohort from raw MIMIC-IV 3.1, both model exporters, and the fairness-audited cross-model evaluation harness — machinery landed and verified in code.
- **Planned (before mid-2027 submission):** Delphi-vs-SurvivEHR head-to-head numbers on the shared cohort (retrains pending); counterfactual and across-layer analyses; additional-cohort comorbidity analyses; substrate extension to medications/measurements.
- **Preliminary (may change):** the exact analysis set beyond the head-to-head (PRS, privacy, stratified-conditioning analyses are ideas in the TODO, not commitments).
- **Known caveats to disclose, not hide:** the judge-side open findings are documented by design (the interpreter sees mover names before writing the summary; the oracle is judged on the set it was handed — the ceiling can sit near 1.0); the scored MIMIC cohort is the long-span (~19%, chronic) subpopulation and every results caption must say so; current substrate scope is first-occurrence diagnoses + death + demographic covariates — a deliberate, documented reduction relative to what SurvivEHR natively consumes.
